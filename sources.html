Source-First Reporting

Sources & Method

On This Day TV is built as a history newsroom—not a trivia feed. The automatic workflow finds the issue, verifies the page, separates historical reporting from later evidence, and publishes a source record with every story.

Calculate the eras

Research the exact date 200, 100, and 75 years ago.

Find actual issues

Identify newspapers that were publishing on that date before searching for a comparison.

Scan and discover

Use archive search and OCR for discovery; OCR alone is never treated as proof.

Verify on the page

Confirm publication, issue date, page, article type, wording, and surrounding context.

Compare carefully

Compare the same event across presses when the evidence supports it. Unrelated stories remain separate.

Publish the source card

Store the publication, city, date, page, archive, URL, translation notes, rights status, and confidence.

Core archive spine

National & early American

Library of Congress Chronicling America, Directory of U.S. Newspapers in American Libraries, American Antiquarian Society, state newspaper programs, university special collections, and original newspaper backfiles.

Black Press

Howard University Moorland-Spingarn / Black Press Archives, Schomburg Center, Library of Congress African American newspaper resources, Amistad Research Center, California Eagle and Los Angeles Sentinel archival collections.

Immigrant, Indigenous & language press

Recovering the U.S. Hispanic Literary Heritage, Hoji Shinbun, Immigration History Research Center Archives, American Jewish Historical Society, Native newspaper guides and tribal archives, plus state and university digitization programs.

Search discipline: “Not found in the issues searched” is acceptable. Inventing a headline, publication, page number, archive, quotation, or URL is not.
